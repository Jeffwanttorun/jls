Zhu Xi is often introduced through philosophy. In Wuyishan, his story also has a physical setting: a river landscape, places of study, inscriptions, and teaching.

[UNESCO’s account of Mount Wuyi](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/911/) connects the Nine-Bend River area with the development and spread of Neo-Confucianism. A recent scholarly study describes Zhu Xi’s long relationship with the Wuyi Mountains through education, writing, inscriptions, and other cultural work.

## What Neo-Confucianism means here

“Neo-Confucianism” is an English academic term for a broad and debated intellectual tradition. It should not be presented as a single set of slogans created by one person.

Zhu Xi drew together and developed ideas from earlier thinkers. The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zhu-xi/) describes his work on the Confucian classics and the wide influence of his thought in China, Korea, Japan, and Vietnam. Those places did not simply receive an unchanged package. Thinkers and institutions adapted, debated, and sometimes opposed his ideas in different settings.

## Wuyi Jingshe needs a careful explanation

The place known as **Wuyi Jingshe** is central to the planned article, but its English description should not be chosen by literal translation alone. Depending on context, sources use words such as academy, retreat, or study center. The final wording should explain what the place was used for and why it mattered, then give the verified Chinese name beside it.

Before publication, the article should confirm:

- the preferred English and Chinese names;
- the construction date and Zhu Xi’s role;
- what teaching or writing can be tied to the site;
- which visible structures are original, reconstructed, or later additions;
- how later institutions in East Asia used or changed Zhu Xi’s ideas.

> **Draft boundary:** Keep the history complex. Do not describe Zhu Xi as the sole creator of Neo-Confucianism, and do not claim that East Asia was shaped in one uniform way. Site dates, names, surviving remains, and lines of influence all require source review.
