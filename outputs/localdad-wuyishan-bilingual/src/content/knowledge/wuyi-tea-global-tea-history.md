Wuyi tea is local, but its history does not stop at the mountains.

The first part of the story is the relationship between environment and skill. Research on tea in northern Fujian describes quality as something shaped by both the growing place and the knowledge of tea workers. That is more useful than treating “rock tea” as a mysterious flavor that comes from stone by itself.

## Several tea histories, not one

Wuyi Rock Tea, oolong tea, and black tea are related subjects, but they are not interchangeable names. Their processing histories, trade histories, and modern categories need separate explanations.

The same care is needed with the word **Bohea**, which appears in European tea history. Scholarly work connects the term with tea imported from China and with the history of Wuyi tea, but the spelling, meaning, and range of teas covered by the word changed across time and sources. The finished article should show that change rather than offering one neat definition for every century.

Modern tea history adds another warning. A study of Gold Jun Mei in Tongmu treats it as a modern tea shaped by older techniques, local knowledge, business decisions, and new markets. A newer product can be connected with tradition without being presented as unchanged ancient history.

## Keep three kinds of content separate

- **Tea heritage** explains processing, terminology, trade, and cultural exchange with sources.
- **Visitor tea guides** help travelers taste, ask questions, and understand what a shop is offering.
- **Local tea stories** introduce real growers, makers, families, and working lives with their permission.

These pages can link to one another, but one should not replace the others.

> **Draft boundary:** Before publication, verify every origin date, trade route, processing claim, and connection between Wuyi tea and Europe or North America with more than one appropriate source. Do not repeat tea legends as history.
