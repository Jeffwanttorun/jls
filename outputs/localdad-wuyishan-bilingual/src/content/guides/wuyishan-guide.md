Wuyishan is a small city in northern Fujian Province, in southeastern China. If you’re looking at a map, it sits inland between Fuzhou and the Jiangxi border. It’s best known for the Wuyi Mountains, Jiuqu Stream, and the roasted oolong tea grown in the rocky hills around town.

A visit to Wuyishan usually centers on walking, riding a bamboo raft, drinking tea, and spending a couple of days away from a packed schedule.

> My simple advice: give Wuyishan at least two full days. Three days feels much more relaxed.

## Wuyishan or Wuyi Mountain?

You’ll see both names in English. **Wuyishan** is the city and the surrounding area. **Wuyi Mountain** usually means the mountains or the protected scenic area. Most visitor hotels and restaurants are in Sangu, next to the park, rather than in the older downtown area.

## When should you visit?

Spring is green and rainy. Summer is hot, humid, and busier while schools are out. Fall usually brings the easiest hiking weather. Winter is quiet, but mornings can feel damp and chilly.

Rain is part of life here. Bring shoes with good grip and a light rain jacket. Weather can change quickly around the mountains.

## Getting here and getting around

Most travelers arrive by high-speed train. Pay close attention to the station name: Wuyishan North and Nanpingshi are different stations and are not equally close to every hotel. Ask your hotel which station is easiest before you book. Taxis, ride-hailing apps, and local buses can get you around once you arrive.

The Main Scenic Area has its own shuttle buses, but you’ll still do plenty of walking between stops.

## A simple first trip

1. Spend one day around Tianyou Peak and Jiuqu Stream.
2. Use another day for the Da Hong Pao area, a tea walk, or a quieter trail.
3. Leave enough time to sit down for tea and have an unplanned meal.

## A practical note

You won’t find English everywhere, so save your hotel name and key destinations in Chinese on your phone. A translation app helps. Most people will try to help, even if the conversation takes a little patience on both sides.
