The Wuyishan Main Scenic Area is the large protected park next to Sangu, the main visitor district. Most of the places you’ve probably seen in Wuyishan photos are inside it. The park has multiple entrances, internal shuttle buses, and separate walking areas, so it doesn’t work like one trail with a single starting point.

You don’t need to cover the whole park in one day. Trying to do it usually turns the visit into a race.

## Tianyou Peak

**Tianyou Peak** is the high viewpoint many first-time visitors ask about. From the top, you can look down on several bends of Jiuqu Stream and see how the river sits between the peaks. The climb is mostly stone steps, and some sections feel steep and exposed. I’d go early in hot weather. If stairs aren’t right for you, skip it without feeling that you missed the whole point of Wuyishan.

## Jiuqu Stream

**Jiuqu Stream**, or Nine-Bend Stream, winds between the sandstone peaks. The traditional bamboo-raft ride is a relaxed way to see the cliffs from below and gives tired legs a break. Raft trips depend on weather, water conditions, schedules, and ticket availability, so check current details before building your day around one. The separate [Jiuqu Stream location guide](/locations/jiuqu-stream) covers family pacing, photography, maps, and questions to confirm before you go.

## Da Hong Pao area

The **Da Hong Pao** area is a narrow valley of cliffs, paths, and working tea gardens. The protected mother bushes connected with the Da Hong Pao story are here, but I think the walk itself does a better job of showing how closely Wuyishan tea is connected to this landscape.

## How to plan your day

- Wear shoes that feel good on stairs and have decent grip.
- Carry water, but don’t pack more than you want to carry up a lot of steps.
- Allow more time than the map suggests.
- Avoid combining every steep climb in one day.
- Check current ticket, shuttle-bus, and rafting rules with an official source before your visit.

> When people try to check off every named sight, they often spend more time watching the clock than looking around. I’d choose two main parts of the park and leave room to look around on foot.

## With children or older family members

Jiuqu Stream and shorter valley walks may work better than a steep climb. Families should build in bathroom, snack, and rest stops. See my [family travel notes](/family-travel) for more practical ideas.
