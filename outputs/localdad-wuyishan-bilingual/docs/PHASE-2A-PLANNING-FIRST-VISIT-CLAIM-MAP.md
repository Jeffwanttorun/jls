**Purpose:** Define the article's future evidence and editorial boundaries before any body copy is written.

This document maps planned claims; it does not approve wording or supply travel facts. A planned statement may enter the article only after its required evidence or firsthand confirmation is available.

## Claim categories

- **A — Stable fact:** A factual statement that is unlikely to change frequently but still needs a traceable authoritative or reliable source.
- **B — Editorial guidance:** Advice based on clearly stated reasoning. It must not be presented as an official rule or universal recommendation.
- **C — Dynamic information requiring verification:** Information that may change, including transportation, tickets, opening hours, access rules, accommodation, weather impacts, and operating conditions. It needs a current official source and a visible reconfirmation boundary.
- **D — Jeff local experience:** A personal observation that may be used only after Jeff explicitly confirms the experience, scope, and appropriate wording. It must remain distinct from official information.

## Risk scale

- **Low:** Limited consequence if wording is misunderstood; still subject to normal editorial review.
- **Medium:** Could mislead trip planning or overgeneralize local experience.
- **High:** Could cause a visitor to lose time, money, access, or make an unsuitable plan.
- **Critical:** Safety-sensitive or likely to create serious practical harm. No Critical claim is currently planned; if one emerges, it requires a separate safety review.

## 3. Risk areas

### Transportation

Transportation is high risk because station names, routes, frequencies, transfers, service areas, and operating times can change. The article must not copy schedule information from a general travel page or preserve temporary information as evergreen advice. Arrival transport, local transport, and scenic-area transport need separate evidence.

### Tickets and opening hours

Prices, ticket combinations, reservation rules, required documents, sales channels, validity, operating hours, last entry, and closures are dynamic. The guide should emphasize what to verify and where responsibility lies; it should not become a frozen ticket manual.

### Accommodation

The article should not contain hotel lists, rankings, prices, availability claims, or endorsements. Even a statement that an area is “convenient” needs a defined activity and a verified transport or location basis.

### Seasons and weather

Long-term climate context and short-term travel conditions are different evidence classes. Avoid “best time” claims. Forecasts, alerts, water conditions, heat, rain, and closures require current verification close to travel.

### Visitor recommendations

Editorial guidance must explain its reasoning and remain optional. Jeff's advice belongs in Category D only after he personally confirms it. Practical examples inside a recommendation may still require official or other verified evidence.

### Family, mobility, and accessibility

Suitability is person-specific and conditions can change. Avoid broad claims such as “easy,” “safe for children,” or “accessible” without precise verified context.

## 4. Evidence rules for drafting

- Do not begin with a source list and assume every source supports every claim.
- Map each factual sentence to the narrow scope of a checked source.
- Record access dates for dynamic official information.
- Keep official facts, editorial advice, and Jeff's firsthand perspective visibly distinct.
- If current information cannot be verified, omit the claim or turn it into a clearly worded question for the visitor to confirm.
- Do not convert a general travel recommendation into Jeff's personal experience without his explicit confirmation.
- Do not use tourism marketing pages, hotel sales pages, or anonymous summaries as primary evidence for operational claims.
