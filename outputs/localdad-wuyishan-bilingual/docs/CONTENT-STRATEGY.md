# Content Strategy

## Long-term purpose

The site is a global English-language introduction to Wuyishan. Its primary mission is to help international visitors understand why Wuyishan matters to the wider world. Practical travel information and personal stories support that mission; neither defines the site on its own.

The site should feel like a person living in Wuyishan is helping one curious visitor understand his hometown. It should never read like a translated tourism brochure.

## Content hierarchy

### 1. Why Wuyishan?

This is the primary pillar. It provides sourced context for Wuyishan's significance beyond trip planning. No article should be published until its claims and sources have been reviewed.

Its four future categories are:

- Natural Heritage: biodiversity, natural history, plant research, specimen collection, and documented scientific exchange.
- Cultural Heritage: Zhu Xi, Neo-Confucian culture, and documented historical influence.
- Tea Heritage: Wuyi tea, oolong tea, black tea, and documented connections with global tea history.
- Living Heritage: how people have lived with the landscape.

These are structural categories, not permission to make claims. All four use one knowledge collection so that their shared editorial and sourcing rules stay consistent. Natural Heritage can still apply stricter specialist review without requiring a separate technical system.

### 2. Explore Wuyishan

This is the experience layer. It answers, "How can visitors experience Wuyishan?" Places, routes, established travel guides, family information, and outdoor experiences belong here. Practical claims that can change must be dated and reconfirmed.

### 3. Stories of Wuyishan

This is a supporting layer. People and interviews should help explain larger themes in Wuyishan rather than become the site's primary definition of the place. Identity, quotations, photographs, video, and publication permission must be confirmed before publication.

### 4. About Jeff

This explains who is behind the site and why he created it. Jeff is someone who lives in Wuyishan and helps international visitors understand the place. His everyday life, family perspective, coffee cart, and firsthand observations define his role here; his guide license is supporting context rather than the site's purpose.

## Public navigation

The primary navigation now follows the long-term hierarchy:

- Home
- Why Wuyishan
- Explore Wuyishan
- Stories of Wuyishan
- About Jeff

The established content fits within that hierarchy as follows:

- Start can become the entry point to Why Wuyishan.
- Guides, Family Travel, Locations, and future Routes belong under Explore Wuyishan.
- Rock Tea may lead to Tea Heritage explanations and practical tea-related experiences without forcing both purposes into one page.
- People, interviews, and local stories belong under Stories.
- About remains the route to About Jeff.
- Contact should remain a secondary utility rather than a strategic pillar.

Do not move existing URLs merely to match a label. Preserve working URLs and use section hubs or redirects when the transition becomes useful.

## Homepage direction

The homepage begins with understanding Wuyishan before arrival. Its hierarchy is:

1. a concise explanation of the site's purpose;
2. a reviewed entry point into why Wuyishan matters;
3. practical ways to explore;
4. supporting people and stories;
5. About Jeff and the editorial standards.

Each homepage section links to a public pillar or existing published content. Do not add empty directories or fictional entries merely to make a section look full.

## Language and evidence

The permanent English editorial standard is `docs/ENGLISH-CONTENT-STYLE.md`. It applies to website copy, metadata, articles, interviews, video descriptions, transcripts, and future scripts. `CONTENT-GUIDE.md` adds Jeff's established voice and prohibited brand language.

- Write natural American English for English-speaking visitors.
- Adapt Chinese concepts for the context instead of translating words, labels, or sentence structure literally.
- Prefer context, explanation, curiosity, respect, and specific firsthand experience.
- Avoid generic tourism marketing and claims such as `must-see`, `hidden gem`, and `ultimate guide`.
- Never invent facts to complete a page or content field.
- Distinguish personal experience, field checks, official information, and facts that should be reconfirmed.
- Knowledge articles should identify their sources. A source list does not by itself verify a claim; editorial review is still required.

## Relationships

Content relationships are typed rather than inferred. Articles can link to places, people, stories, topics, and videos. Videos can relate to a place, person, story, topic, or route. This permits future chains such as Person → Village → History topic → Route → Video without asserting that any unreviewed relationship is true.

People stories should connect back to a larger theme whenever the source material supports that relationship. A profile is not published merely to add a personal story; it should help explain Wuyishan's culture, tea, communities, landscape, or change over time.

## Topic system

Heritage themes use canonical IDs from `src/data/topics.ts`. Editors select an existing ID instead of typing a label, which keeps terminology consistent across knowledge articles, places, interviews, and videos. Adding a topic to the registry defines vocabulary only; it does not create a public page or establish a factual relationship.

## Source verification

- Record enough source information for another editor to identify it, including title, publisher when known, URL when available, and access date for web sources.
- Prefer primary sources and appropriate scholarly or official material for scientific, historical, and cultural claims.
- Do not treat a source list as proof that every claim is supported. Review the relationship between each important claim and its source before publication.
- Keep Jeff's firsthand observations distinct from historical, scientific, and official claims.
- Recheck translations, names, dates, quotations, and claims about international connections with appropriate source material.
- Leave a claim unpublished when its evidence or interpretation is uncertain.
