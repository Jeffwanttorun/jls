## Verification standard

This record separates four kinds of statements:

- **tea agriculture facts**, which require regional agronomy, tea science, meteorological, or technical evidence;
- **tea heritage facts**, which require documented cultural, craft, or transmission evidence;
- **visitor guidance**, which must not imply that a tea garden is publicly accessible or that one rule applies to every site; and
- **general interpretation**, which may explain why a topic is useful but must remain clearly interpretive rather than scientific proof.

A study of one cultivar, experimental plot, planting system, season, or tea garden is not treated as evidence for every tea garden in Wuyishan. An official news report is useful for documented local practice but is not treated as equivalent to peer-reviewed research. A heritage source about processing does not automatically verify cultivation conditions or visitor access.

### TG-S1 — The taste of tea: Material, embodied knowledge and environmental history in northern Fujian, China

- **Title:** The taste of tea: Material, embodied knowledge and environmental history in northern Fujian, China
- **Author:** Kunbing Xiao
- **Publisher / institution:** *Journal of Material Culture*, SAGE Publications, Volume 22, Issue 1
- **URL / DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1359183516633901
- **Publication date:** First published online 2016-05-26; journal issue published in 2017
- **Access date:** 2026-07-15
- **Supports:** TG-01 (background only), TG-02, TG-05; contextual support for TG-04
- **Exact supported scope:** The publisher abstract concerns Mount Wuyi rock tea in northern Fujian. It states that local evaluation of tea-growing sites considers the material qualities of tea plants and tea, and that specialist workers' skills and techniques, together with terrain and environment, are regarded as important to tea quality. It frames this combination as embodied cultural knowledge.
- **Limitations:** The full article was not openly available during this check, so the verified scope is limited to the publisher's abstract. It does not verify the article's climate, rainfall, sunlight, or seasonal-observation checklist. It does not support visitor access, visitor learning outcomes, or garden boundaries.

### TG-S2 — Seasonal Dynamic Characteristics of Soil Physical and Chemical Properties and Enzyme Activities of Different Planting Patterns in the Wuyishan

- **Title:** 武夷山不同种植模式下茶园土壤理化性质和酶活性的季节变化特征 / Seasonal Dynamic Characteristics of Soil Physical and Chemical Properties and Enzyme Activities of Different Planting Patterns in the Wuyishan
- **Authors:** Wang Feng, Chang Yunni, Sun Jun, Wu Zhidan, Chen Yuzhen, Jiang Fuying, and Yu Wenquan
- **Publisher / institution:** *Journal of Tea Science* (茶叶科学), 2024, Volume 44, Issue 2, pages 231–245
- **URL / DOI:** https://doi.org/10.13305/j.cnki.jts.2024.02.004
- **Publication date:** 2024-04-15; page release date 2024-04-30
- **Access date:** 2026-07-15
- **Supports:** TG-02, TG-03, TG-04
- **Exact supported scope:** The study compares forestland, conventional tea gardens, and organic tea gardens in the Wuyishan tea area. Samples collected in May, August, November, and February show that planting pattern, season, and their interaction relate to measured soil properties and enzyme activities. It directly documents that human cultivation choices and seasonal conditions are relevant to tea-garden soil systems.
- **Limitations:** This is a soil and enzyme study, not a complete account of tea plant development, farmer knowledge, local communities, or visitor experience. It does not verify rainfall, sunlight, leaf observation, or universal management behavior. Its results should not be generalized beyond the studied sites, planting patterns, measurements, and seasons.

### TG-S3 — Exploring the Impact of Tea/Trachelospermum jasminoides Intercropping on Soil Health and Microbial Communities

- **Title:** Exploring the Impact of Tea (*Camellia sinensis* (L.) O. Ktze.)/*Trachelospermum jasminoides* (Lindl.) Lem. Intercropping on Soil Health and Microbial Communities
- **Authors:** Yulin Xiong, Shuaibo Shao, Dongliang Li, He Liu, Wei Xie, Wei Huang, Jing Li, Chuanpeng Nie, Jianming Zhang, Yongcong Hong, Qiuling Wang, Pumo Cai, and Yanyan Li
- **Publisher / institution:** *Agronomy* (MDPI), 2024, Volume 14, Issue 6, Article 1261; authors affiliated with Fujian Agriculture and Forestry University and Wuyi University
- **URL / DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy14061261
- **Publication date:** 2024-06-11
- **Access date:** 2026-07-15
- **Supports:** TG-02, TG-04
- **Exact supported scope:** At one experimental Rougui tea garden in Wuyishan City, the study records a subtropical monsoon climate with abundant rainfall and red acidic soil, and compares intercropping with monoculture. It finds that the tested intercropping system changed measured soil nutrients, enzyme activity, and microbial-community characteristics. This supports the bounded proposition that tea-garden soil conditions are affected by both environmental setting and cultivation decisions.
- **Limitations:** The work covers one site, one cultivar, and one specific intercropping experiment. Its climate and soil description is a study-site description, not proof that every Wuyishan garden has identical conditions. It does not verify a general sunlight claim, visitor learning, local community relationships, or access rules. Some broad background statements in the article rely on other cited studies and should not be treated as findings of this experiment.

## Claim-to-source mapping

### TG-01 — A tea garden helps visitors understand where tea begins and how tea connects with landscape and people

- **Mapped evidence:** TG-S1 supports the relationship between Wuyi tea, environment, and specialist human skill. TG-S2 through TG-S4 provide bounded evidence for cultivation conditions and human management. TG-S8 confirms that organized tea-learning activities have taken place in named Wuyishan settings.
- **What is verified:** There is real agricultural and cultural material that a structured experience can explain, and organized tea-learning activities exist in some identified settings.
- **What is not verified:** That any tea garden is publicly accessible, that merely visiting a garden produces understanding, or that all garden experiences include landscape, community, and farming interpretation.
- **Decision:** **Partly supported as interpretation.** Use conditional language and require a verified site, permission, and learning context before presenting this as a visitor experience.

### TG-02 — Tea gardens are shaped by both natural conditions and human work

- **Mapped evidence:** TG-S2 compares human planting systems and seasonal soil changes. TG-S3 compares human cultivation treatments at a Wuyishan site within a defined climate and soil setting. TG-S1 describes Wuyi tea quality knowledge as combining environment and specialist skill.
- **What is verified:** Defined Wuyishan tea-garden conditions reflect both environmental setting and cultivation or production decisions.
- **What is not verified:** A single uniform relationship applying identically to all Wuyishan gardens, all communities, or every stage from cultivation through processing.
- **Decision:** **Supported in a bounded form.** Keep the central relationship, with variation by site, stage, and practice made clear.

### TG-03 — Tea growing involves attention to seasons, weather, and plant conditions

- **Mapped evidence:** TG-S2 directly studies seasonal differences. TG-S4 documents local monitoring of weather, microclimate, growth condition, frost, drought, and seasonal management decisions.
- **What is verified:** Seasons, weather conditions, and observed plant growth are used in documented Wuyishan tea research and management programs.
- **What is not verified:** The current implied routine for every tea worker, or the exact checklist “weather, leaf condition, seasonal timing, and plant needs” across all gardens.
- **Decision:** **Supported in a bounded form.** Attribute the idea to documented research and management contexts, and preserve “practices vary by garden and season.”

### TG-04 — Local growing conditions influence tea development

- **Mapped evidence:** TG-S2 supports soil, season, and planting-pattern effects within studied sites. TG-S3 documents one Wuyishan site's climate, rainfall, acidic soil, and cultivation treatment. TG-S4 documents local monitoring of temperature, humidity, rainfall, light, elevation, growth, frost, and drought.
- **Factor review:**
  - **Climate / temperature:** Supported as a monitored and locally relevant factor by TG-S3 and TG-S4.
  - **Rainfall / water stress:** Supported in defined local monitoring and drought contexts by TG-S3 and TG-S4.
  - **Sunlight / light:** TG-S4 verifies that light is monitored in Wuyishan tea gardens and considered in growth and quality-difference analysis. It does not establish a simple universal effect. **Needs wording reduction** if presented as a proven general rule.
  - **Soil conditions:** Supported by TG-S2 and TG-S3 for measured soil properties and specific planting systems.
  - **Surrounding vegetation:** TG-S3 supports one intercropping treatment; it does not support a general claim about all surrounding vegetation. **Needs wording reduction.**
- **Decision:** **Partly supported.** Replace an undifferentiated factor checklist with a bounded explanation that local research and monitoring consider soil, season, weather, and selected site conditions. Do not imply identical effects across gardens.

### TG-05 — Tea gardens are connected with tea-making knowledge and local practices

- **Mapped evidence:** TG-S1 supports the Wuyi-specific combination of environment, specialist skill, and embodied knowledge. TG-S5 supports plantation-management, picking, processing, and knowledge transmission at a China-wide heritage level. TG-S6 documents Wuyi Rock Tea processing technique, transmission, training, and teaching.
- **What is verified:** Wuyi tea is connected to specialist production knowledge, and documented heritage systems connect plantation management, picking, processing, and transmission.
- **What is not verified:** That the same person or community performs every stage, that every garden follows one tradition, or that a processing standard proves specific cultivation practices.
- **Decision:** **Supported in a bounded form.** Separate cultivation facts from processing and heritage facts, and identify the scope of each source.

### TG-06 — Visitors can understand tea better by seeing where tea grows

- **Mapped evidence:** TG-S8 shows that organized tea-learning activities can be built around instruction and experience. TG-S1 through TG-S6 supply the agricultural and cultural context that such instruction may explain.
- **What is verified:** Structured, permission-based education can provide tea context.
- **What is not verified:** A measurable learning outcome from simply seeing a garden, universal access, or a standard visitor program.
- **Decision:** **Needs wording reduction.** Retain only as an explicitly interpretive and conditional statement, not a factual promise.

### TG-11 — Tea experiences should respect working tea gardens and local boundaries

- **Mapped evidence:** None of the checked scientific, standards, heritage, or official program sources sets universal visitor-boundary rules for Wuyishan tea gardens.
- **What is verified:** No specific garden is established by these sources as publicly accessible. Site-specific permission and rules therefore cannot be assumed.
- **What is not verified:** A universal path rule, touching rule, operating schedule, visitor entitlement, or Wuyishan-wide garden code.
- **Decision:** Keep only as **cautious editorial visitor guidance**: ask permission, follow the responsible person's instructions, and do not imply access. If a real garden is later added, record its owner or operator, access conditions, rules, and check date.

## Special review findings

### Climate, rainfall, sunlight, and soil

- **Climate and rainfall:** Verified as documented characteristics or monitored variables in defined Wuyishan studies and programs, not as one uniform formula for all gardens.
- **Sunlight:** The checked China Meteorological Administration report confirms that light is monitored and compared with tea growth and quality data. No peer-reviewed Wuyishan source checked here establishes the article's broad sunlight implication. If kept, it **needs wording reduction** to a monitored site condition.
- **Soil:** Strongest support comes from TG-S2 and TG-S3, but both have defined study designs and cannot represent every garden.
- **Surrounding vegetation:** One intercropping study supports the effect of one selected companion plant under one experiment. The broad phrase **needs wording reduction** unless more regional evidence is found.

### Tea cultivation knowledge

- Research and official reporting support seasonal monitoring, weather-risk response, planting choices, and management decisions in defined Wuyishan contexts.
- Cultural sources support specialist knowledge and transmission, especially in processing.
- The article must not merge research-led management, individual practitioner experience, and traditional processing knowledge into one universal farming method.

### Tea-garden visitor boundaries

- No checked source verifies a universal public-access system or visitor code.
- Permission and respectful conduct should remain clearly labeled general guidance.
- Any future practical information needs direct confirmation from the garden's responsible person and a “last checked” date.

## Sources that should not be used for the priority claims

### UNESCO World Heritage Centre — Mount Wuyi

- The existing article candidate is useful for broad World Heritage context.
- It should **not** be used as evidence for climate, rainfall, sunlight, soil conditions, cultivation knowledge, visitor learning, or tea-garden boundaries.
- Its discussion of tea production impacts and property management does not establish the article's specific agricultural or visitor claims.

### Tourism marketing pages, commercial tea-company pages, and itinerary pages

- These were not used.
- They may describe access or experiences offered by a particular operator, but they cannot serve as scientific evidence. If a future place page names an operator, its own current rules may be used only for that site's access details and should be dated.

### Draft text of the replacement Wuyi Rock Tea national standard

- A draft consultation document was located, but it was not used as evidence.
- Draft wording should not be treated as the authoritative final text. The published GB/T 18745-2026 replacement is not effective until 2027-04-01, while the accessible official record for the current 2006 standard does not expose enough technical text to verify the priority claims.

### Generic tea-science statements from studies outside Wuyishan

- These were not used to fill gaps in the regional evidence.
- General tea-plant responses elsewhere cannot automatically establish the conditions or practices of Wuyishan gardens.
