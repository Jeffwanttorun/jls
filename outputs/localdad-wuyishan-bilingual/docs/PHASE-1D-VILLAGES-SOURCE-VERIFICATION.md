## Verification standard

A source is recorded only when it is traceable and its relevant scope was checked. A source can support only the wording and geographic or social scope that it documents. General research about rural China is not treated as evidence about Wuyishan.

Visitor guidance is separated from factual claims about villages. Future People and Story work is also separated from current evidence because interviews and local stories do not yet exist in the content system.

## Claim ID alignment

The requested claim summaries are used below. In the earlier Batch 1 source mapping, VW-14 was phrased as a conclusion about communities continuing to change. The present review also considers the requested interpretation that villages can help visitors understand communities and relationships with place. Evidence for community–landscape relationships does not prove that every visit will produce that understanding.

## Claim-by-claim mapping

### VW-01 — Villages as places where people live within the landscape

**Finding:** Supported with geographic limits.

UNESCO records inhabited villages within the World Heritage property, while the park regulation and zoning documents recognize resident living and production areas. The *Land* study provides contemporary research on park–people relationships among tea-farming residents.

**Useable scope:** Villages and resident communities within the documented World Heritage, biosphere-reserve, national-park, or scenic-area contexts.

**Limitation:** The evidence does not justify one description for every village in the wider Wuyishan administrative area. References to “traditions continuing” need a named practice or a more specific source.

### VW-03 — Landscape, settlement patterns, and local activities

**Finding:** Partly supported; broad wording needs reduction.

The cultural-landscape study associates river systems and topography with landscape stratification. The Xiamei planning approval provides a concrete example of settlement along waterways in a mountain basin and a historical connection with tea trade. Park sources document resident production zones and tea or bamboo activities.

**Useable scope:** The landscape is associated with settlement form and local activities in documented examples.

**Needs wording reduction:** Avoid stating that geography determines where all communities developed, how all residents worked, or which industries became important. Use “can influence,” “is associated with,” or a named example.

### VW-05 — Tea as a connection between villages and landscape

**Finding:** Supported for some communities.

UNESCO, the park zoning information, the *Land* study, the tea-industry paper, and the Xiamei approval document connections among tea production, tea livelihoods, protected landscapes, and particular communities or places.

**Useable scope:** Tea connects some documented communities with land, livelihood, production, and heritage.

**Limitation:** Cultivation, processing, trade history, family life, and identity are distinct claims. One source should not be treated as evidence for all of them, and the claim must not be generalized to every village.

### VW-11 — Villages as living communities

**Finding:** Suitable as an editorial interpretation, with contextual evidence.

The official and academic sources show that residents live and work in these protected landscapes and participate in conservation and community governance. This supports treating villages as communities with resident agency rather than as visual objects.

**Useable scope:** A modest editorial statement that visitors are entering places where people live and work.

**Limitation:** “Not tourist scenery” or “not a theme park” is not a sourced local rule. Keep the tone respectful and factual.

### VW-12 — Respectful interaction rather than photography alone

**Finding:** No Wuyishan-specific source verifies this as a local custom or official rule.

**Useable scope:** General editorial guidance may recommend respecting privacy, asking before photographing identifiable people, avoiding intrusion into private space, and following any posted or host-provided rules.

**Needs wording reduction:** Do not imply that residents are available for conversation, that interaction is always appropriate, that photography is prohibited, or that one behavior standard applies to every village.

### VW-14 — Understanding communities and relationships with place

**Finding:** Partly supported as an interpretive purpose, not a guaranteed visitor outcome.

Research on residents’ ecosystem perceptions, local conservation participation, cultural landscapes, and tea livelihoods demonstrates that community perspectives can deepen understanding of relationships with place.

**Useable scope:** Village context and responsibly produced resident stories can help visitors ask better questions and understand that landscapes are also lived places.

**Limitation:** A casual village visit does not automatically provide this understanding. Future People and Story content will require real interviews, informed consent, accurate attribution, and specific local context.

## Existing candidate source decision

### “Research on Cultural Landscapes and Rural Communities”

**Decision:** Remove from the article’s future verified source list.

The recorded item has no author, publication title, institution, date, URL, DOI, ISBN, or other traceable identifier. Searches for the exact title and close variants did not identify a publication that could be matched responsibly. It must not be cited.

**Verified replacements by scope:**

- Tu and Zhang for Wuyishan National Park cultural-landscape structure;
- He and Su for park–people relationships and tea-farming residents;
- the Xiamei protection-plan approval for one documented settlement and tea-history example;
- the national-park regulation and zoning page for resident living, production, village planning, and community-governance context;
- You et al. for resident participation and perceptions in heritage conservation.

These sources are not interchangeable. Each should be cited only for its recorded scope.

## Evidence categories kept separate

### A. Verified village and community facts

- Inhabited villages and resident production or living areas exist within documented protected-area contexts.
- Tea and bamboo activities are documented in identified communities or park zones.
- Xiamei provides a traceable example of settlement form and tea-trade history.
- Academic work documents resident perceptions, tea-farming livelihoods, and conservation participation in defined study areas.

### B. Visitor guidance

- Respect for privacy and resident agency is editorial guidance.
- Site-specific access, photography, and behavior rules require confirmation from the relevant community, property owner, operator, or official authority.
- Visitors should not be promised interaction or access.

### C. Future People and Story direction

- Future resident stories can explain community–landscape relationships only after real reporting or interviews.
- Interviews require consent, accurate attribution, dates, locations, and a clear distinction between one person’s experience and a broader community claim.
- Future editorial plans are not evidence for the current article.

## Claims that still need wording reduction before publication

1. VW-01: narrow “traditions continue” unless a named practice is verified.
2. VW-03: avoid broad causal wording about settlement, work, and industries.
3. VW-05: keep “some villages” and separate cultivation, processing, trade, livelihood, family life, and identity.
4. VW-11: retain as a calm respect principle, not a contrast or official rule.
5. VW-12: make privacy, permission, and non-intrusion conditional; do not imply expected conversation.
6. VW-14: present learning as an opportunity, not an automatic result of visiting.
7. Remove or postpone statements about younger generations, generalized new businesses, changing lifestyles, and adapting traditions until evidence is specific.
