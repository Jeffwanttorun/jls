## Claim-number note

The task describes **TC-09** as the claim that tea traditions continue while adapting to modern life. In `docs/PHASE-1D-BATCH1-SOURCE-MAPPING.md`, that idea is currently recorded as **TC-10**. The mapped TC-09 in that document concerns the relationship between environment and tea-making technique.

This verification follows the meaning requested in the task and labels it **TC-09 (requested; TC-10 in the existing mapping)**. The underlying claim IDs should be reconciled in a separate documentation task so future editorial work does not apply evidence to the wrong sentence.

## Evidence boundaries

The checked sources cover three different kinds of information:

1. **Wuyishan-specific cultural and material research** — the Kunbing Xiao article directly studies rock tea in the Mount Wuyi area.
2. **China-wide tea practices** — UNESCO's intangible-heritage record describes processing knowledge and social practices across China. It does not prove that every listed practice occurs in every Wuyishan tea community.
3. **Contemporary safeguarding and visitor activity** — official Fujian and standards records document training, transmission, regulation, and organized cultural experiences. They do not establish how every family, business, visitor, or tea table behaves.

Visitor interpretation and guide advice must remain clearly separate from historical or cultural claims. Suggested questions for visitors may be useful editorial guidance, but they are not evidence that a practice is universal or locally expected.

## Verified source records

### TC-S1 — Traditional tea processing techniques and associated social practices in China

- **Title:** Traditional tea processing techniques and associated social practices in China
- **Publisher / institution:** UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage
- **Author:** No individual author listed
- **URL:** https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-tea-processing-techniques-and-associated-social-practices-in-china-01884
- **Publication date:** Inscribed in 2022; an exact webpage publication date is not stated
- **Access date:** 2026-07-15
- **Supported Claim IDs:** TC-01, TC-04, TC-07, TC-09 (requested; TC-10 in mapping)
- **Exact supported scope:** The UNESCO record describes a China-wide intangible-heritage element covering knowledge and skills in plantation management, leaf picking, manual processing, drinking, and sharing tea. It also describes tea as part of daily life and socialization, including greeting guests and building relationships within families and among neighbours, and records transmission through families and apprenticeships.
- **Limitations:** This is not a study of Wuyishan alone. It cannot establish that every listed social practice is common in Wuyishan, or that the article's examples involving friends, business, and family all occur with the same frequency or meaning locally. The UNESCO record should support only the China-wide context unless paired with Wuyishan-specific evidence.

### TC-S2 — Decision of the Intergovernmental Committee: 17.COM 7.b.2

- **Title:** Decision of the Intergovernmental Committee: 17.COM 7.b.2
- **Publisher / institution:** UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, Intergovernmental Committee for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage
- **Author:** No individual author listed
- **URL:** https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/17.COM/7.b.2
- **Publication date:** 2022
- **Access date:** 2026-07-15
- **Supported Claim IDs:** TC-01, TC-04, TC-07, TC-09 (requested; TC-10 in mapping)
- **Exact supported scope:** The decision records knowledge, skills, social practices, daily-life use, socialization, family and community transmission, master–apprentice transmission, formal education, and use of social media for exchange and dialogue within the nominated China-wide element.
- **Limitations:** The decision evaluates the national nomination; it does not provide Wuyishan-specific ethnographic detail. It also warns about over-commercialization. It should not be used to present all modern commercial activity as cultural continuity or to treat organized visitor experiences as proof of everyday local practice.

### TC-S3 — The taste of tea: Material, embodied knowledge and environmental history in northern Fujian, China

- **Title:** The taste of tea: Material, embodied knowledge and environmental history in northern Fujian, China
- **Publisher / institution:** *Journal of Material Culture*, SAGE Publications, Volume 22, Issue 1, pages 3–18
- **Author:** Kunbing Xiao
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1359183516633901
- **Publication date:** First published online 2016-05-26; issue published March 2017
- **Access date:** 2026-07-15
- **Supported Claim IDs:** TC-01, TC-03, TC-07, TC-09 (requested; TC-10 in mapping)
- **Exact supported scope:** The publisher abstract directly concerns rock tea in the Mount Wuyi area. It connects local evaluation of tea-growing sites with terrain, the material qualities of tea plants and tea, specialist worker skills and techniques, embodied cultural knowledge, and vocabulary for judging taste and fragrance. It also describes how recent commercial quality standardization had to accommodate the cultural importance of this traditional knowledge.
- **Limitations:** Full text is access-restricted, so this verification uses the publisher's abstract and bibliographic record. It does not verify detailed claims about tea-table conversation, greeting guests, family routines, business discussion, or the precise process by which makers learn through observation. Those points need full-text verification or another suitable local source.

## Claim-to-source mapping

### TC-01 — Tea culture is connected with people, knowledge, landscape, and experience

- **Mapped evidence:** TC-S3 directly connects Mount Wuyi rock tea with terrain, specialist workers, skills, techniques, embodied knowledge, taste, and fragrance. TC-S1 and TC-S2 provide broader China-wide evidence for tea knowledge, drinking, sharing, socialization, and transmission.
- **Supported:** The people–knowledge–landscape connection is supported, especially in relation to tea production and evaluation.
- **Not fully supported:** The article's wider claim about everyday relationships throughout Wuyishan is not established by the Wuyishan-specific abstract.
- **Decision:** **Needs wording reduction.** Keep the Wuyishan-specific landscape, skill, and embodied-knowledge relationship. Attribute wider social-practice context to Chinese tea traditions rather than all Wuyishan communities.

### TC-03 — Tea is connected with local knowledge and tea-making practices

- **Mapped evidence:** TC-S3 directly documents specialist skills, technique, environmental knowledge, and embodied cultural knowledge in Mount Wuyi rock-tea production and evaluation. TC-S5 confirms a current formal standard for traditional Wuyi Rock Tea processing.
- **Supported:** Wuyishan rock tea is connected with local environmental assessment, specialist skill, processing technique, and embodied knowledge.
- **Limitation:** Do not extend this to every grower, maker, family, or tea practice without evidence.
- **Decision:** **Supported within the checked Wuyi rock-tea scope.**

### TC-04 — Tea experiences can include tasting, conversation, and understanding local context

- **Cultural evidence:** TC-S1 and TC-S2 support tea drinking, sharing, socialization, greeting guests, and relationship-building at the China-wide level. TC-S4 documents organized tea-culture learning and experience activities in Wuyishan.
- **Visitor interpretation:** Tasting, listening, and learning about origin or production can be presented as ways a visitor may approach a tea experience. This is editorial interpretation, not proof of a fixed local custom.
- **Guide advice:** Suggested questions can remain optional guidance after Jeff reviews them. They should not be described as required etiquette or as questions every host expects.
- **Not supported:** “Discuss business” in Wuyishan tea communities is not supported by the checked sources. The full list of welcoming guests, meeting friends, business discussion, and family time is not verified as a Wuyishan-specific pattern.
- **Decision:** **Needs wording reduction.** Remove or postpone unsupported examples and clearly label visitor suggestions as guidance.

### TC-07 — Tea making involves observation, experience, and continued practice

- **Mapped evidence:** TC-S1 and TC-S2 document knowledge, skills, family and apprenticeship transmission, and formal education. TC-S3 documents specialist skills and embodied cultural knowledge in Mount Wuyi rock tea. TC-S4 documents current training and transmission, and TC-S5 confirms an official standard covering traditional processing and training.
- **Supported:** Tea making involves learned skills, embodied knowledge, transmission, training, and continued practice.
- **Not fully supported:** The accessible evidence does not directly verify “observation” as the specific Wuyishan learning method or every item in the article's list of growing plants, seasons, processing, and recognizing characteristics.
- **Decision:** **Needs wording reduction** if “observation” or the full knowledge checklist remains. Keep only the skill, experience, processing, sensory evaluation, and transmission elements directly supported.

### TC-09 (requested; TC-10 in mapping) — Tea traditions continue while adapting to modern life

- **Mapped evidence:** TC-S3 documents traditional embodied knowledge interacting with recent national commercial quality standardization. TC-S1 and TC-S2 document contemporary transmission through family, apprenticeship, formal education, and digital exchange. TC-S4 documents current safeguarding, training, institutions, cultural exchange, and organized learning activity in Fujian and Wuyishan. TC-S5 confirms current standardization and training scope.
- **Supported:** Traditional tea knowledge continues through several documented contemporary mechanisms and interacts with standards, institutions, education, training, and exchange.
- **Not fully supported:** “Modern life” is too broad, and the sources do not show that all farmers, families, businesses, or communities adapt in one shared way.
- **Decision:** **Needs wording reduction.** Name the documented mechanisms of continuity and change rather than making a general claim about adaptation.
