# Writing for Local Dad Jeff

This site should sound like Jeff explaining Wuyishan to one curious visitor—not a tourism bureau, travel agency, or generic travel blog.

Read this voice guide together with `docs/ENGLISH-CONTENT-STYLE.md`. The English style guide defines the permanent editorial method, cultural-adaptation rules, content-type boundaries, evidence standard, and read-aloud test; this file defines Jeff's established voice and specific brand language.

## The basic voice

Write in clear, conversational American English. Be warm, but do not sell. Use the first person when Jeff has a real local observation to share.

Prefer contractions and everyday verbs when they sound natural: `you’ll` instead of `you will`, `doesn’t` instead of `does not`, and `works for families` instead of `is suitable for families`. Read every paragraph out loud. If it sounds like a brochure, school essay, or translated notice, rewrite it.

Treat “Local Dad Jeff” as the brand name, not as a literal identity label to repeat in normal sentences. In body copy, introduce Jeff naturally: “I’m Jeff — a dad, coffee-cart owner, and licensed tour guide living in Wuyishan.” Adapt Chinese concepts for English-speaking readers instead of preserving Chinese word order or internet labels.

When a Chinese term has no exact English equivalent, explain what it means in context. Do not force a word-for-word translation into quotation marks as if it were a familiar English expression.

Good:

> I’d go early in hot weather. If stairs aren’t right for you, skip the climb without feeling that you missed the whole point of Wuyishan.

Avoid:

> Tianyou Peak is a must-visit attraction with breathtaking views you will never forget.

## What every article should include

- Enough background for someone who has never heard of Wuyishan
- A practical question the article answers
- Specific local details: place names, terrain, weather, transportation, tea customs, food, or daily routines
- Honest limits, tradeoffs, or uncertainty
- A clear distinction between Jeff’s opinion and a fact
- A reminder to verify details that can change, such as tickets, schedules, prices, and park rules

## Write from real knowledge

Use personal phrases only when they are true:

- “I often notice…”
- “When friends ask me…”
- “As a dad…”
- “I’d choose…”
- “Around my coffee cart…”
- “In rainy weather here…”

Never invent a conversation, family memory, customer story, route experience, or recommendation just to make an article sound personal. If Jeff has not confirmed an experience, write the known facts plainly or ask him for details.

## Language to avoid

Do not use:

- Top 10
- Must visit or must see
- Hidden gem
- Paradise
- World-famous scenic wonder
- Breathtaking
- Bucket list
- Best ever
- Something for everyone
- Unforgettable experience
- Ultimate guide
- Once-in-a-lifetime
- Generic pace-based travel slogans

Do not use pace as brand language, a mood shortcut, or a generic travel philosophy. Say what the visitor will actually do or gain instead: leave room between activities, sit down for tea, avoid a packed schedule, spend more time by the river, or choose fewer parts of the park.

Also avoid empty claims such as “stunning scenery” when a specific description would be more useful. Say what a visitor can actually see, hear, taste, or expect.

## Keep recommendations honest

Avoid telling every reader that the same place is right for them. Mention stairs, heat, rain, crowds, travel time, language limits, and child-friendly pacing when relevant.

Jeff is improving his English and preparing to guide international visitors in the future. Describe that stage consistently, and only publish specific guiding arrangements after Jeff confirms them.

## Before publishing

Ask:

1. Could this paragraph appear on any destination website? If yes, make it specific to Wuyishan.
2. Does this sound like an advertisement? If yes, remove the sales language.
3. Is the personal experience true and confirmed by Jeff?
4. Would a first-time foreign visitor understand the local background?
5. Are changing details clearly marked for verification?
